## 2. Places where I cannot tell how a result was obtained

- **"prices each constraint at equal risk, through the risk row's multiplier" (Sec. I).** The costs actually come from each constraint's µk at a fixed σm. λ* sets up P-MI and A1. As written, it sounds as if λ* prices the other constraints.
- **The I1 bound-selection rule ("among the families with the largest median C1 elasticity").** It does not say how many families, why Mom is at 0.025 specifically, or whether Mom = 0.025 applies to every I1 case or only when Mom is the perturbed bound.
- **I2 (K = 67, K = 79).** The f behind these K values is never stated, and nothing explains why 2009-03 is dropped.
- **"consistent with outer approximation stopping early".** This rests on bound equality at 29/36 with "its logs are off". "Stopping early" is an inference, and the 7 mismatches are not discussed.
- **"(within 5.0×10⁻³|V| of the plain relaxation at 33)".** I cannot tell what quantity is within that tolerance.
- **"polished values tie at all 144".** The tolerance for a tie is not given.
- **The volatility drift "(computed in N3 from the stored solutions)".** It is unclear how root-only N3 probes produce I1/I2 volatilities.
- **The kink flag ("flagged as a kink if |s+ − s−|").** It is defined in III-D, but no kink results are reported anywhere.
- **N2(b) "scaling the objective by 10³".** Elsewhere s_obj = 10⁴; the change is not explained.
- **The s_obj rule ("brings the pilot's proxies… to 9.1–21") and "median 1.12 units/s".** The pilot is never described.
- **"integrality, not the step, causes the errors".** This is an inference from the convex baseline. It does not explain why the errors then depend on δ, which is the next sentence.
- **Coincidence to verify.** At δ = 10%, the fixed-integer median 0.023 and the big-M median 0.073 are the same for all 23 cases and for the 2017-12 subset (n = 11). This is possible, but please check that the abstract's "0.016 against 0.023; big-M 0.073" is the 2017-12 subset and not the pooled numbers.

## 6. Three questions I would ask in Q&A

1. P-MI fixes λ* and its volatility drifts (2.22–3.31%/yr in N1). So the Q2 "marginal costs" are not at equal risk, which is the premise of the paper. Would re-root-finding λ for each P-MI solve change which estimator wins?

3. With n = 23 (and n = 11 at 2017-12), is "the best estimator depends on the step" more than noise? Is there a paired comparison of perspective versus fixed-integer errors (0.013 vs 0.023)?
