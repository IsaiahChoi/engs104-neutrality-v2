## 1. Verdict: MOSTLY self-contained

The model (1)–(2), the data and risk-model construction, and derivations A1–A9 and III-D can be followed with ENGS 104 background. Every derivation I checked holds. I could recompute almost every count (108, 2040, 312, 288, 144, 164, 147, 24, 65, 252), so the experiment design adds up.

## 4. Derivations

All of these check out:
- A1: the λ_min bound 7.5 × 10^-11 = 10^-6 · (0.03/√12)².
- A2–A3: the formulas for ν, λ, A*_eq and TC.
- A4: ΣD^-1α⊥ = α⊥, the vanishing cross term, and √(1−φ).
- A5: concavity and monotonicity of the perturbation function.
- A6.
- A7: big-M slope a − 2λdb; perspective z = w/ℓ, q = ℓw, slope a − λdℓ; the inequality for ℓ/2 < b < ℓ.
- A8: the interval (3) and the certified-nonconcavity test V(b^(k−1)) + V(b^(k+1)) > 2Ū_eff(b^(k)).
- A9: 10^-6/ℓ² = 0.16 and 10^-6(0.06/0.0025)² = 5.76 × 10^-4.
- III-D: η = ε/δ_r; s+ − s− has coefficients −6, 4, 4, −1, −1, summing to 16 in absolute value, so the bound is 8η.
- The P1 argument (fixed active set gives an affine solution, a quadratic value, and an exact central secant).

Gaps and wording problems:
- **A2–A3**: "A*_eq = α⊥w* = σm√(g⊤Σ^-1 g)" skips the step α⊤Σ^-1 g = g⊤Σ^-1 g, which follows from AΣ^-1 g = 0. Add one line.
- **A1**: "Slater's condition holds…, so KKT is sufficient" is off. KKT is sufficient for any convex problem. Slater gives necessity and strong duality, which you use in "Conversely, by strong duality…". Reword.
- **A1**: the root's existence also needs volatility to fall below σm as λ → ∞ (w(λ) → 0, since 0 is feasible). This is unstated.
- **A4 vs C1/Fig. 2**: A2–A4 assume "no costs and no other rows". C1's idealized mandate (ii) adds dollar neutrality, so the closed form does not apply to it. "each with the risk budget only" does not clearly say the costs are off. State which mandate Fig. 2's "full factor neutrality" is, and that costs are zero there.
- **A9**: the 0.16 and 5.8 × 10^-4 figures assume Gurobi applies an absolute 10^-6 to w² − qζ exactly as written. This is asserted, not shown. A cited solver-manual statement would close it.
- **A9 / IV-C**: the "outer approximation stopping early" mechanism is inferred ("its logs are off") and never explained. A reader cannot see why an unconverged outer approximation gives a root bound looser than the plain relaxation (a polyhedral superset gives a larger upper bound). One sentence would fix this.
- **III-D**: the authors correctly say that the duality-gap test does not prove the ε premise, because R is evaluated after resetting q. Fine as written.
- "A2–A3" is a single block, so I cannot tell which part is A2 and which is A3.
